# chanterelle mushroom

## Core idea
The chanterelle contains 8 essential amino acids, 19 free amino acids, 21% protein, vitamins A, C, and D2, cibaric acid, 10 fatty acids, potassium, iron, citric acid, malic acid, ergosterol (which turns into vitamin D2 when exposed to UV light), 3 free sugars, 6 volatile aroma compounds, and the fragrance compound benzaldehyde. Notably, chanterelles contain a significant amount of ergosterol, which regulates calcium transport in the human body. This vitamin D2 also acts as a rodenticide that causes calcium accumulation in mammals, birds, and fish. This high vitamin D2 content explains why slugs, snails, or insects do not eat chanterelles. Indeed, when foraging for chanterelles, you rarely see any slug bite marks. Additionally, the volatile aroma compound 1-octene-3-ol, which is also found in pine mushrooms, acts as an insect repellent that drives away slugs, and this same compound is naturally present in chanterelles.
