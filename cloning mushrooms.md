# cloning mushrooms

## Core idea

Cloning mushrooms lets you propagate a mushroom indefinitely. 

You will need: 
* A healthy, fresh mushroom (ideally one that hasn't fully dropped spores yet)
* Sterile scalpel or razor blade, alcohol (isopropyl 70%+), lighter/torch to flame-sterilize
* Petri dishes with agar malt extract agar or PDA (potato dextrose agar)
* A clean, low-draft workspace — a [[still-air box (SAB)]] or [[flow hood]] if you have one; even a clean room with doors/windows closed cuts contamination a lot
* Nitrile gloves, mask helps too

Steps:
1. Prep the mushroom. Choose one with a firm, dense stem — that's where you'll take tissue from, since it's less exposed to airborne contaminants than the cap surface.
2. Sanitize your space and tools. Wipe down the work surface with alcohol. Flame the scalpel until glowing, let it cool a few seconds.
3. Split the mushroom. Tear (don't cut — tearing exposes cleaner, unhandled tissue) the mushroom lengthwise through the stem to expose the interior.
4. Take the tissue sample. With the flamed scalpel, cut a small cube (roughly 2-3mm) from the interior of the stem, near the base where it meets the cap — this inner tissue is the most sterile part of the mushroom.
5. Transfer to agar. Open the petri dish lid just enough to slide the tissue onto the agar surface, then reseal quickly. Work fast and keep the dish tilted away from your breath.
6. Label and incubate. Mark the dish with species/date, then store somewhere dark and stable, around 70-75°F (21-24°C).
Watch for growth. Within a few days to a week you should see white mycelium radiating out from the tissue chunk. This confirms a successful clone.
1. Check for contamination. Green, black, or bright pink fuzzy growth = mold/bacterial contam — discard. Clean white, rope-like mycelium = good.
2. Transfer/isolate. Once mycelium colonizes a good portion of the plate, cut a clean section (avoiding any contaminated edges) and transfer to a fresh agar plate to purify the culture. Repeat once or twice until you have a clean, vigorous plate.
3. Move to grain spawn. Once you have a healthy, pure culture, you can transfer a wedge of colonized agar to sterilized grain (rye, wheat berries, or millet) to build up spawn mass, then eventually to your bulk substrate (supplemented sawdust for shiitake).


A [[flow hood]], or a still-air box (a plastic tote with armholes cut in, sealed with a lid) is a cheap, effective way to cut contamination way down.
Shiitake specifically can be a bit slower and pickier to grow out on agar than something like oyster, so patience helps.
