# cybernetics

## Core idea
Systems thinking
- feedback, control
- homeostasis
  - The Antagonistic Control Rule: Homeostasis cannot rely on a single control knob. It requires opposing forces working at the same point 
  - The Error-Controlled Set Point: A homeostatic system does not actually measure "safety." It measures the difference (error) between the current environmental state and its internal target. The system only springs into action when this error margin gets too wide. 
- allostasis: The cybernetic principle of "achieving stability through change."
- regulation
  - The Law of Regulatory Selection (The First Law of Cybernetics): A regulator can only achieve stability by restricting or selecting from the total possible variety of the system. Regulation is effectively the filtering or dampening of unwanted chaos.
  - Error-Controlled Regulation: Also known as negative feedback. The regulator only acts after a disturbance has pushed the system away from its target goal (e.g., a home thermostat)
  - Cause-Controlled Regulation: Also known as feedforward control. The regulator anticipates disturbances by measuring them before they hit the system, executing defensive actions in advance.The
  - Regulatory Paradox: To successfully regulate a system, the regulator must absorb or destroy the variety of the environment. However, if it over-regulates, it destroys the internal variety (adaptability) the system needs to survive. 
- recursion
  - Beer-Heisenberg Principle: A cybernetic rule stating that the more precise a measurement of a system is at one level of recursion, the less certain you can be of its status at a higher level.
- black box: Treating a system by looking only at its inputs and outputs, without opening it up to see inside.
- variety
  - Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety: A control system must have at least as many internal states (flexibility) as the environment it wants to regulate.
- viability
  - Stafford Beer’s Viable System Model (VSM). It dictates that for any natural or artificial system to survive independently in a changing environment, it must possess five specific, interconnected subsystems.
