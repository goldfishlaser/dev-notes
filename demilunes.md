# demilunes

## Core idea
rainwater harvesting technique used to slow down water runoff, fight erosion, and restore degraded or dry land

semi-circular pit with the open side facing uphill, you trap flowing water, forcing it to slowly sink into the soil to nourish crops, trees, or native plants. 

Tools:
- A frame or line level (to find the landscape's contour line)
- Measuring tape or a pre-cut rope (for drawing the radius)
- Marking tools (chalk, flour, or wooden stakes)
- A sturdy shovel or pickaxe
- Compost or organic fertilizer (roughly 35 kg / 75 lbs per demilune)
- Large stones or rocks (optional, to reinforce the downslope rim)

1. Map the Contour and Mark the Line. Locate the slope of your land. Use an A-frame or line level to mark a level contour line perpendicular to the flow of downhill water.Decide on your dimensions. Standard agricultural demilunes typically have a diameter of 2 to 3 meters (6.5 to 10 feet). 
2. Outline the Half-Moon. Drive a stake into the exact center of your contour line. Tie a rope equal to your desired radius (e.g., 1 to 1.5 meters) to the stake. Swing the rope downhill to trace a clean, semi-circular crescent. Crucial Alignment: The straight, flat "open" side of the half-moon must face directly uphill to catch the oncoming water.
3. Dig the Basin. Dig out the interior of your marked crescent to a depth of 6 to 12 inches. Throw all the loose soil to the downhill outer edge of the crescent. This creates a raised earthen barrier (a berm or bund) that stops the water from escaping. 
4.  Build and Reinforce the Berm. Pack down the excavated soil on the outer rim firmly so it doesn't wash away during a heavy storm. Line the outer, curved edge of the berm with large stones to add structural strength.
5.  Leave a small gap or lower point at the very tips of the crescent's "horns." This acts as a safe overflow spillway during torrential rains so your main wall doesn't blow out. 
6.  Amend the Soil. Mix about 35 kg (approx. 75 lbs) of compost, manure, or organic matter directly into the bottom of the basin.
