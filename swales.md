# swales

## Core idea
Swales are shallow, level trenches dug along a land’s contour, with excavated soil piled on the downhill side to form a berm.  They function as rainwater harvesting devices that slow, spread, and sink runoff into the soil, thereby preventing erosion, recharging groundwater, and irrigating plants passively. 

Mark the contour: Use an [[A Frame Level]] to identify a level line across a gentle slope (ideally 3–15% grade); avoid steep slopes where stability is compromised. 
Excavate the trench: Dig a U-shaped trench 1–3 feet deep and 1–4 feet wide along the marked contour, piling the removed earth immediately on the downhill side to create the berm. 
Ensure levelness: The trench bottom must be perfectly level so water spreads evenly; if drainage is the primary goal, add a slight 1–2% grade to direct water to a safe exit point like a rain garden. 
Plant for stability: Immediately plant the berm with deep-rooted trees, shrubs, or perennials to anchor the soil and prevent erosion, while mulching the trench to enhance water infiltration.
Maintain regularly: Clear debris, silt, and obstructions every 3–4 months and after heavy storms to ensure the swale continues to function without blocking or overflowing.
